Tap Memory Ring — the product story

Trial version: remembers your spot, not the temperature · mechanical-only v1

One-line goal

Make a cheap little ring that clips onto a normal single-lever tap so you can save your favourite water spot once, and feel a click every time you find it again — no plumber, no electronics.

Why no temperature in v1

The ring sits on the dry collar (T2), behind the water. Output temperature depends on the hot-supply temp, the cold-supply temp, and the lever position — and the device can only know the lever position. The other two drift with the seasons, so a saved position is a saved spot, not a guaranteed degree. We say that honestly in the marketing.

Why it's worth building

Single-lever mixers are the most common tap type and the click/detent mechanism itself is proven, cheap engineering. The real gap is that nobody sells this as an external, retrofit clip-on — every click-and-stop mechanism that exists today is built into the tap at the factory.

The honest risks

One ring fitting every tap is the hardest engineering problem (expect an adapter kit, not one part for all). A saved spot is not an exact temperature. And a large incumbent (Moen, Delta, Kohler) could copy a proven idea — speed and price are the defence.

The hardest job is making one ring grip many collar sizes. Five realistic ways, judged the same way a drill chuck, camera aperture, ski-boot dial, hose clamp and jar-opener each solve a version of this puzzle.

Soft liner + dial v1 pick

A soft silicone/TPE liner hugs the collar like a foam pole-grip, tightened tool-free by a small dial. Cheapest, widest size range, gentle on chrome. Best for taps with a stationary collar; on a rotary shower valve (like the photo) we instead anchor to the fixed wall plate — see the 3D view.

Band clamp (worm-drive)

A band cinches shut with a small screw, like a garden-hose clip. Very cheap, huge range — but can mark the tap finish and needs a tool. Good fallback if liner costs run high.

Three-finger grip (jaw chuck)

Three jaws slide in together, like a drill chuck holding a bit. Strong and precise, but bulky and has more parts than a light clip-on ring needs.

Camera-eye ring (iris)

Overlapping leaves close like a camera aperture or the pupil of an eye. Looks premium and smooth, but many thin parts make it fragile and pricey to mould for v1.

Squeeze sleeve (collet)

A slotted tube squeezes shut, like pinching a straw. Very precise — but the size range it can cover is under ~1mm, so it only works as a fixed-size adapter, not a universal fit.

Roadmap — cheapest & safest first

1. Validate demand — talk to 20–30 people, would they pay $20–40?
2. Confirm the collar-stays-still assumption on several real tap brands.
3. CAD model + one 3D-printed sample to hold and click.
4. File a low-cost provisional patent once the design is real.
5. Design-for-manufacturing, soft tooling, small pilot run.
6. Production tooling and launch.

The golden rule

Do not spend tooling money until real people on real taps confirm the fit and the click. Tooling is where the big cost starts — everything before it should stay cheap.

One-breath pitch

You know how you always fiddle with the shower to get it warm? This little ring clips onto your tap. Set your spot once, and after that it clicks when you're back on it. No plumber, fits most taps.
